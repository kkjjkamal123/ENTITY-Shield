1. Problem and approach

Scam SMS (fake KYC, electricity cut-offs, courier "customs", "digital arrest", UPI refund tricks, part-time job scams) reach every Indian phone, and the people most at risk are the least able to spot them. Cloud scanners need the message to leave the phone. ENTITY Shield does all of it on the phone's Arm CPU:

  • Two-tier design. Small ExecuTorch detectors (130 KB each) score every SMS, link and network event in milliseconds and decide the verdict. Llama 3.2 1B on ExecuTorch only explains a flagged item, in plain English or Hindi.
  • Llama can't overrule or invent. The verdict and the remediation steps are not generated. The app writes the start of Llama's answer (verdict prefill) and gives it a short fact sheet, so it explains why.
  • Phone-only network protection. A local DNS-only VPN sees every app's lookups (attributed per app) and a LAN tripwire catches devices probing the phone. That covers the network-intrusion and malware-behaviour parts of PS3 with no extra hardware.

2. Architecture

Inputs: SMS_RECEIVED, inbox, share/select-text/Link Guard, the DNS VPN and the tripwire → detectors (SMS, URL, behaviour rules) → verdict (safe / suspicious / dangerous) → instant notification with fixed steps → when the user opens the alert, Llama streams the explanation. Coexistence rules:

  • Detection never waits for Llama.
  • Llama loads on demand and frees its ~1.3 GB after 90 s.
  • Llama runs only while an alert is on screen.
  • The fixed system prompt is pre-filled into the KV cache when an alert fires (ExecuTorch prefillPrompt).

Latency budget. The warning is instant; the explanation follows on the user's screen. The grey bar is the mistake we measured and removed (§6).

3. Detectors

Features: hashed character 2–5-grams (32,768 slots, FNV-1a) plus 15 named signals for SMS and 11 for URL. The signals include urgency, OTP/PIN requests, payment requests, threats, prize and job lures, authority claims, KYC/Aadhaar, brand-vs-domain mismatch, shorteners, and sender is a 10-digit mobile number vs a registered DLT header such as VM-HDFCBK. Model: logistic regression, exported as one Linear + sigmoid ExecuTorch program (XNNPACK). Data: UCI SMS Spam Collection, plus 101 hand-written Indian templates (47 scam families, 54 legitimate: bank OTP and debit alerts, deliveries, bills, DLT promotions, personal Hinglish/Hindi). For URLs: PhishTank (time split), OpenPhish, Tranco. The test sets hold out whole templates and the newest phishing.

Same answer on the phone. The app's Kotlin featurizer is a line-by-line port of the Python one. The golden vectors pass in Python, on the JVM (unit tests) and on the phone through the production path: SMS 43/43, URL 40/40, max difference 6×10⁻⁷. Retraining on Windows reproduced the Mac model bit-exactly (max difference 0.0).

Faster by measuring. 98% of detection time was featurizing: a String per n-gram, plus a fresh 131 KB dense vector copied into a tensor on every call. An allocation-free featurizer and one reused direct FloatBuffer per ExecuTorch model (Tensor.fromBlob) took the full SMS check from 9.4 ms to 5.0 ms p50 and the URL check from 2.7 to 1.1 ms on the Dimensity 7300, with parity unchanged.

4. Network Guard: network intrusion and malware behaviour, on the phone

  • DNS-only local VPN. Only the fake resolver address is routed into the TUN interface, so app traffic never flows through Shield. Each question is parsed (IPv4/UDP/DNS, unit-tested) and attributed to its app with getConnectionOwnerUid. It is scored on the phone (allow-lists, then the URL model), then either forwarded to the network's own resolver or answered NXDOMAIN if dangerous.
  • Malware behaviour (per app):
    • Beacon: 6 or more lookups of one domain at a near-constant interval.
    • DGA burst: 8 or more different non-existent names within 60 s.
    • DNS tunnel: 15 or more unique subdomains, or long high-entropy labels.
    Each becomes an alert naming the app, with "remove app" as the action.
  • LAN tripwire. Decoy listeners on 2222, 2323, 5555, 8080, 8443 and 9999. Any contact means another device on the Wi-Fi is probing the phone: a scan (3 or more ports within 30 s), password guessing (5 or more connections to one port), or an ADB-worm probe (port 5555).
  • Test app. beaconsim imitates a C2 beacon, a DGA burst and DNS exfiltration using only DNS lookups of made-up names, so the alerts name a real installed app.

5. Llama 3.2 1B on ExecuTorch: the explanation layer

Model: Meta's quantization-aware-trained Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct-QLORA_INT4_EO8, exported with ExecuTorch 1.5.1 (XNNPACK, 4-bit groupwise weights, 8-bit dynamic activations, KV cache, custom SDPA, context 2048; 1.12 GB). It runs in the APK through LlmModule. It was chosen over our own 8da4w export (faster) because it never refused and wrote fluent Hindi in our October tests. Prompt: fact sheet (KYC = Know Your Customer; banks never ask for OTP/PIN by SMS link; a UPI PIN only sends money; police never arrest over video calls; helpline 1930) + the detector's evidence + verdict prefill. Without the prefill the 1B refused to explain scams at all.

Pre-warming the system prompt

When an alert fires, the fixed 99-token system prompt is pre-filled into the KV cache (LlmModule.prefillPrompt), so opening the alert costs only the message's own 134 tokens. In the app on the Infinix (foreground, same alert, greedy), the first token arrives after 1.00 s pre-warmed vs 1.78 s cold (−44%). The pre-warmed answer matches the cold one for its first 14 tokens, then picks an equally correct wording. This is consistent with a near-tie flipping under a different floating-point summation order when the prompt is prefilled in two chunks; we have not confirmed it directly. Removing the literal BOS token from the prefilled text diverged from the first sentence, so prefillPrompt adds no BOS of its own.

Three findings.
  1. Android's background cgroup moves ExecuTorch to the little cores. Our first build ran Llama on a THREAD_PRIORITY_BACKGROUND thread. /proc/<pid>/task/*/cgroup showed every XNNPACK worker in cpuset:/background: prefill 25 tok/s and a 9.2 s first token on the Dimensity 7300. The little-core row above reproduces the effect (3.9× slower prefill). Llama now runs at normal priority, only while its alert is on screen.
  2. ExecuTorch's thread heuristic picks 3 threads on the Dimensity 7300, because midr_el1 is unreadable and it falls back. On the Snapdragon, 3 instead of 4 big-core threads costs 17% of prefill.
  3. ENTITY's llama.cpp result holds on ExecuTorch and on Armv9 (A720/A520, i8mm). 8 threads cost 19% decode. Pinning to the big cores gives the fastest and steadiest prefill (±0.6 vs ±6.6 tok/s).

8. Privacy, security and limits

No account, analytics or cloud call. Messages, links and lookups are judged on the phone. The INTERNET permission is used only to forward the phone's own DNS lookups to its usual resolver. The inbox audit exports counts only. Debug hooks exist only in debug builds. Full table: docs/PRIVACY.md.

  • Network Guard sees this phone's own traffic, not other devices. Strict Private DNS or in-app DNS-over-HTTPS bypasses its DNS view.
  • Test data: the Indian SMS test set is templated. The real-inbox audit is the trust number (pending).
  • URL recall is 0.66 on the time-split feed: hostname only, by design.
  • Llama 1B is wrong 10–15% of the time on hard inputs, so it explains but never decides.

9. Phase 2 plan

  • Network:
    • A full-tunnel flow meter with NetFlow features.
    • A flow intrusion model on NF-UNSW-NB15-v2 / NF-CSE-CIC-IDS2018-v2, with a cross-dataset test.
    • A per-app anomaly baseline (ExecuTorch on-device training, TrainingModule).
  • Llama: distilled English/Hindi explanation data and LoRA on the QAT checkpoint (as "Llama-…" per the licence); prefix-KV reuse; Tamil.
  • Coverage: WhatsApp/Telegram notifications, call screening ("digital arrest"), UPI QR checks, a field trial with 10–20 phones.
  • Arm: the KleidiAI on/off ablation (custom AAR), a Mali GPU (Vulkan) delegate for the detectors, and upstreaming the ExecuTorch Windows flatc lookup fix and the core-count heuristic fix.
